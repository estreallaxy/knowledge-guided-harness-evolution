                                              A BSTRACT
         Test-time scaling has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enhancing the reasoning
         capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by allocating additional computa-
         tional resources during inference. However, this paradigm is inherently inefficient
         due to the generation of different reasoning traces, leading to significant computa-
         tional overhead. Speculative decoding offers a promising avenue for mitigating this
         inefficiency, yet its efficacy in the structured and repetition-rich context remains
         unexplored. To bridge this gap, we introduce the first comprehensive benchmark
         designed to evaluate speculative decoding methods in LLM test-time scaling.
         Our benchmark provides consistent experimental protocols across representative
         test-time scaling paradigms (e.g., Best-of-N sampling and multi-round thinking),
         enabling a fair comparison of three major categories of speculative decoding:
         model-based, training-based, and N-gram-based methods. Extensive experiments
         reveal that simple N-gram-based methods effectively capture repetitive patterns,
         demonstrating unique potential in accelerating test-time scaling. This phenomenon
         demonstrates the value of integrating N-gram-based methods with model-based
         or training-based approaches to benefit both repetitive and diverse reasoning in
         test-time scaling. We hope this benchmark spurs further research on speculative
         decoding for test-time scaling, enabling faster and more practical reasoning in
         LLMs through better handling of repetitive and diverse reasoning paths. Code
         available at https://github.com/sunshy-1/SpecTTS-Bench.


1    I NTRODUCTION
In the pursuit of more capable large language models (LLMs), the test-time scaling (Snell et al.,
2025; Wang et al., 2023; Madaan et al., 2023; Tian et al., 2025; Yao et al., 2023) has emerged as
a key performance driver on complex tasks. Unlike training-time scaling, which relies on larger
models or datasets, test-time scaling offers a complementary strategy that can be applied to already-
deployed models without retraining. This approach enhances performance by allocating substantial
computational resources during inference, allowing the model to “think” longer and more deeply.
Dominant paradigms in test-time scaling include Best-of-N (BoN) sampling (Snell et al., 2025) and
multi-round thinking (Tian et al., 2025). These paradigms have demonstrated remarkable success
in complex reasoning (Lightman et al., 2023; Cobbe et al., 2021; Li et al., 2022). However, this
enhanced performance comes at a steep price. Generating multiple full-length responses or iterative
reasoning chains incurs prohibitive computational overhead, leading to a significant latency bottleneck
that severely limits their use in real-time, interactive scenarios.
To unlock the potential of test-time scaling for practical use, it is imperative to mitigate its associated
latency. Speculative decoding (Liu et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025) has emerged as a leading
technique to achieve this goal. This approach accelerates autoregressive generation by using a fast

the larger target model. This process substantially reduces wall-clock time while remaining lossless.
On the other hand, the repetitive nature of test-time scaling creates uniquely favorable conditions for
speculative decoding to excel, particularly for retrieval-based solutions (Hu et al., 2025; Oliaro et al.,
2024). These retrieval-based approaches are inspired by N-gram models, which predict the next token
based on a specific sequence of prior tokens, such as pairs (bigrams) or triples (trigrams). As a model
explores different reasoning paths, it frequently outputs highly repetitive token sequences. These can
include recurring logical phrases (e.g., “Let’s consider the case where...”), standard boilerplate code,
or common transitional expressions (as shown in Table 1). This repetitive structure suggests that
N-gram-based methods (Hu et al., 2025; Oliaro et al., 2024), which dynamically cache and reuse
recently generated token sequences, are exceptionally well-suited to this scenario.
Despite promising acceleration, existing literature lacks comparative analysis to determine which
speculative decoding architecture best exploits computational redundancies in test-time scaling.
Current benchmarks evaluate these methods in generic tasks (Xia et al., 2024), overlooking the unique
characteristics of advanced reasoning paradigms. Consequently, critical questions remain unanswered:
Can a simple, adaptive N-gram mechanism outperform a more complex, pre-trained draft model in a
scenario ripe with repetition? How do the trade-offs between these methods—flexibility, training cost,
and real-time adaptability—manifest when accelerating test-time scaling frameworks? Without a
dedicated benchmark to investigate these specific dynamics, the community cannot make informed
decisions about how to efficiently scale up inference-time reasoning.
To address this critical gap, we introduce the first comprehensive benchmark designed to systemati-
cally compare different speculative decoding methods for accelerating test-time scaling. Specifically,
our work provides a head-to-head comparison of 9 speculative decoding methods (detailed in Section
3.3), covering nearly all state-of-the-art (SOTA) techniques in this domain. We aim to elucidate the
trade-offs among these approaches and, most importantly, test the hypothesis on the unique effective-
ness of using N-gram patterns to accelerate test-time scaling. Our observations are summarized as
follows. (i) The method that relies on training an auxiliary draft model shows promising acceleration
potential, but its inference behavior is inherently coupled to the training process, limiting adaptability
across diverse reasoning scenarios. (ii) Methods leveraging N-gram patterns excel at capturing
redundancy in test-time scaling; in particular, those that store and reuse N-gram patterns during
generation achieve progressive acceleration by reusing past computations, enabling efficiency gains
that accumulate across generation steps. However, this advantage is sensitive to sampling temperature.
(iii) A brand new hybrid method called SAM[EAGLE-3] is tested (detailed in Section 3.3). Hybrid
speculative decoding uniquely combines methods that train an auxiliary draft model for semantic
alignment with those leveraging N-gram patterns to capture repetition, unlocking distinct potential
for reasoning under test-time scaling. However, current hybrid strategies remain heuristic, failing to
effectively harness the semantic richness of draft models and the reusability of N-gram patterns. We
hope these findings can provide practical guidance for the design of speculative decoding strategies
in test-time scaling, balancing adaptability, efficiency, and robustness for complex reasoning tasks.

2    R ELATED W ORK
Test-time scaling for enhanced reasoning. The paradigm of test-time scaling enhances model
performance by allocating more computational resources at inference time. This principle can be
realized through two main avenues (Zhang et al., 2025): (1) specialized training that endows a
model with an inherent capability to dynamically scale its computation, or (2) external, training-free
frameworks that guide a fixed model. The former approach, inherent test-time scaling, is embodied
by the development of “slow thinking” or System 2 reasoning models. Models such as OpenAI o1
series (OpenAI et al., 2024) are explicitly trained to internalize multi-step reasoning. Complementing
this inherent capacity are external frameworks, which apply an outer loop of control to a base model,
further amplifying its reasoning performance without modifying its parameters. This paper focuses
on analyzing two foundational categories of such frameworks: (1) Sampling-based methods such as
BoN sampling (Snell et al., 2025) and Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023). (2) Iterative refinement
methods such as Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) and multi-round thinking (Tian et al., 2025). More
details about these frameworks can be found in Appendix A.1.1.
Speculative decoding for inference acceleration. Speculative decoding has emerged as a SOTA
solution for reducing LLM inference latency losslessly. The landscape of speculative decoding
can be classified into three families: (1) Model-based speculative sampling which uses a smaller,
general-purpose language model as the drafter (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023). (2)
Training-based speculative decoding which trains a specialized draft model (Cai et al., 2024; Li et al.,
2024a; 2025c; Sun et al., 2026). (3) N-gram-based method which builds a “draft” by retrieving token
sequences from a dynamic cache of recent outputs (He et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024; Hu et al.,
2025; Oliaro et al., 2024). While these methods have been benchmarked on generic tasks (Xia et al.,
2024), their interplay with test-time scaling frameworks remains largely unexplored. The highly
structured and often repetitive nature of reasoning chains generated by methods like BoN presents a
unique environment where the trade-offs between these different speculative decoding families are
unknown. Our work directly addresses this gap by systematically evaluating their performance in
these latency-critical, redundancy-rich reasoning scenarios. Additional relevant work is discussed in
Appendix A.1.2.

3.1     B ENCHMARKED T EST- TIME S CALING F RAMEWORKS

This work selects two particularly representative frameworks in test-time scaling, namely BoN and
multi-round thinking, as the basis of our benchmark.

3.1.1    BON
The BoN (Snell et al., 2025) is a foundational technique designed to improve output quality by
leveraging a generate-and-rank mechanism. Instead of relying on a single, potentially flawed output
from the model, BoN operates in two distinct stages:

• Generation: Given a request, the LLM is prompted to generate N independent candidate solutions
  or reasoning paths. This is typically achieved by sampling from the model’s output distribution
  multiple times with a non-zero temperature to ensure diversity. The collection of these N samples
  represents a broad exploration of the potential solution space.
• Selection/Verification: After generating the N candidates, a verifier module is used to score and
  select the best solution. The verifier can range from a simple, heuristic-based checker to a more
  sophisticated, separately trained reward model. In its most canonical form, a process-based verifier
  might assess the correctness of each intermediate step in a solution, rather than just evaluating the
  final answer. The candidate with the highest score is then chosen as the final output.

3.1.2    M ULTI - ROUND T HINKING
Multi-round thinking (Tian et al., 2025) improves reasoning through iterative refinement. This method
is inspired by the human cognitive process of reconsideration. It enhances a model’s reasoning by
prompting it to reflect on and improve its own previous answers over rounds, as follows:

• Initial Response (Round 1): The LLM generates an initial answer to the user’s prompt. This
  represents the model’s initial or intuitive response.
• Iterative Refinement (Rounds 2 to M): The process is repeated for a pre-defined total of M
  rounds. In each subsequent round (from 2 to M ), the model is prompted to refine its previous
  output. The new prompt is constructed by augmenting the original question with the answer from
  the preceding round. For example, the prompt for the 2nd round takes the following structure:
        {Original Question}
        The assistant’s previous answer is:                {Round 1 Answer}
        Please re-answer.
  This mechanism forces the model to reconsider its initial reasoning path, allowing it to self-correct
  errors and elaborate on its response with each iteration.

3.2     B ENCHMARKED DATASETS

Having defined the computational frameworks that expand reasoning effort at inference time, we
now turn to the datasets used for evaluation. The choice of datasets is critical to ensure that our
benchmark measures reasoning capabilities across a diverse set of challenging problems. To this end,
we have selected four widely-recognized benchmarks that have become staples in recent literature for
evaluating the limits of LLM reasoning capabilities. Our evaluation suite is composed of 120 samples
extracted from the following datasets:

• AIME 2024 and AIME 2025: The American Invitational Mathematics Examination (AIME)
  (AIME, 2025) features challenging competition-level mathematics problems that demand creative
  and rigorous problem-solving skills. We use the official problems from the 2024 and 2025
  competitions, utilizing the full set of 30 problems from each.
• MATH-500: A subset of the MATH dataset (Hendrycks et al., 2021), a comprehensive benchmark
  of mathematics problems ranging from pre-algebra to calculus and beyond. It is the standard for
  evaluating mathematical reasoning in LLMs. Given the considerable size of the full 500-problem
  dataset, we selected the first 30 problems to ensure a focused and manageable evaluation.

3.3     B ENCHMARKED S PECULATIVE D ECODING A LGORITHMS

We benchmark a diverse set of methods, which can be broadly categorized into three groups: model-
based speculative sampling, training-based speculative decoding, and N-gram-based methods. This
collection of methods encompasses almost all existing speculative decoding techniques compatible
with reasoning models, capturing the full spectrum of current SOTA solutions. We provide a summary
of the above methods in Table 2. Detailed descriptions of each method are presented below.

• Model-based Method
  • Speculative Sampling (SpS) (Chen et al., 2023): As a pioneering speculative decoding method,
    SpS employs a smaller, faster draft model to generate a sequence of candidate tokens. The larger
    target model then validates these tokens in parallel using a rejection sampling mechanism.

• Training-based Method
  • EAGLE-3 (Li et al., 2025c): This is an advanced speculative decoding approach that dispenses
    with a separate draft model. Instead, it attaches lightweight trained auto-regressive heads to the
    target model’s intermediate layers to generate drafts.

• N-gram-based Method
  • Prompt Lookup Decoding (PLD) (Saxena, 2023): PLD identifies N-grams from the prompt that
    match the sequence generated so far and uses them as speculative candidates. PLD is particularly
    effective in tasks where the output is likely to contain substrings from the input.
  • Retrieval-based Speculative Decoding (REST) (He et al., 2024): REST utilizes a datastore of
    example sequences and a retrieval model to find continuations relevant to the current context.
    These continuations serve as the speculative draft, which is then verified by the target model.
  • Lookahead (Fu et al., 2024): A parallel decoding algorithm generating and validating multiple
    N-grams simultaneously. Unlike single-sequence speculative decoding, Lookahead maintains
    multiple continuations and uses a tree structure to verify candidates in a single forward pass.

    • PIA (Zhao et al., 2024): This method utilizes a trie structure to manage and retrieve contextual
      information from the prompt and decoding context efficiently. It supports tree-based speculative
      decoding, where multiple candidate token sequences are generated and verified in parallel.
    • SAM (Hu et al., 2025): SAM employs a suffix automaton to maintain the contextual history of
      the generated sequence. This data structure allows for the effective identification of previously
      generated token sequences (suffixes) that best match the current context.
    • Recycling (Luo et al., 2025): Recycling enhances speculative decoding by caching the top-K most
      probable subsequent tokens for each token verified by the target model. This cache, constructed
      from the highest logits observed during past generation steps, serves as a high-quality source of
      draft candidates for future occurrences of the same token.

• Hybrid Method
  • SAM with EAGLE-3 (SAM[EAGLE-3]) (Hu et al., 2025): This approach is a hybrid strategy that
    dynamically switches between the SAM and EAGLE-3 frameworks. When SAM identifies a
    matched suffix that is too short to be beneficial for speculation, the system defaults to EAGLE-3.

4.1     E XPERIMENT S ETUP

We investigate the effect of speculative decoding methods in reasoning models DeepSeek-R1-Distill-
Llama-8B (DSL-8B) (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025) and Qwen3-8B (QW3-8B) (Yang et al., 2025),
employing the widely-used test-time scaling paradigms of multi-round thinking (with 2 rounds) and
BoN (with 4 trajectories). We evaluate methods on the reasoning datasets AIME24/25, MATH500,
and GPQA (detailed in Section 3.2). The evaluation pipeline from prior work (Xia et al., 2024) is
adopted to ensure fair comparison. For the reasoning models, we use the float16 data type and apply
greedy/stochastic decoding with a batch size of 1, following the original settings of the evaluated
methods. The evaluation metrics include: Mean Accepted Tokens (MAT), which measures the
average number of tokens accepted per speculative decoding step, and the Walltime Speedup Ratio
(Speed), which quantifies the inference efficiency gain relative to vanilla autoregressive decoding. The
evaluated methods including model-based speculative sampling (e.g., SpS), training-based speculative
decoding (e.g., EAGLE-3), and N-gram-based methods (e.g., PLD, REST, Lookahead, PIA, SAM,
and Recycling). More implementation details are provided in the Appendix A.2.

4.2.2   N- GRAM - BASED M ETHOD — T OKEN N- GRAM
• Among token N-gram methods, which identify repeated patterns at the token level, SAM
most efficiently captures redundant patterns. For token N-gram methods, we assess SAM, PLD,
REST, Lookahead, and PIA. Overall, SAM achieves the best performance among these token N-gram
methods, even rivaling the training-based method EAGLE-3. For instance, under the greedy decoding
setting with multi-round thinking, SAM achieves a 38% higher overall speedup ratio than EAGLE-3
on DSL-8B (2.66×→1.93×). This advantage primarily stems from the high efficiency of SAM in
suffix matching, as well as the repetitive patterns inherent in reasoning traces. Other methods, such as
PLD, PIA, and Lookahead, also demonstrate considerable potential, achieving around 1.5× speedup
across various test-time scaling paradigms. However, due to the higher time complexity of their
N-gram matching mechanisms, they still fall short of SAM in acceleration. REST performs relatively
poorly. We hypothesize this is because the external chat datastore used by REST has limited overlap
with reasoning datasets, resulting in fewer effective token reuse opportunities.
• Sampling temperature significantly affects token N-gram-based methods. We note that token
N-gram methods exhibit notable sensitivity to sampling temperature. Taking SAM as an example,
under the multi-round thinking paradigm, when the temperature increases from 0 to 0.6, its overall
speedup on QW3-8B drops by approximately 22% (from 2.28× to 1.78×), and on MAT it decreases
from 2.37 to 1.96, representing a reduction of about 17%. This highlights a key trade-off: while these
methods are particularly effective at leveraging the repetitive patterns in reasoning model outputs
under test-time scaling, their acceleration benefits tend to decrease as output diversity increases.

  Takeaway 2: Token N-gram methods excel at capturing redundancy in reasoning traces under
  test-time scaling, yet this advantage comes with sensitivity to sampling temperature variations.

4.2.3   N- GRAM - BASED M ETHOD — P ROBABILISTIC N- GRAM
• Probabilistic N-gram method Recycling, which captures the co-occurrence of top-K next
tokens through logits, achieves the highest MAT among N-gram-based methods. As shown
in Table 3, Recycling achieves performance comparable to the strongest retrieval-based method
SAM. Under greedy decoding, Recycling slightly lags behind SAM in speedup ratio, yet attains a
higher MAT. We attribute this to Recycling’s higher computational complexity in draft generation.
Specifically, Recycling employs a tree-structured speculative verification, resulting in significantly

• Probabilistic N-gram method exhibits low sensitivity to temperature sampling. As the tem-
perature increases from 0 to 0.6 in the multi-round thinking paradigm, Recycling’s overall speedup
ratio on QW3-8B decreases only from 2.15 to 2.06—a decline of less than 5%. In contrast, SAM’s

from Recycling’s preservation of approximate next-token prediction logits, specifically the top-k

and the target model’s sampled tokens even as temperature varies.

  Takeaway 3: The probabilistic N-gram method offers superior stability and higher draft to-
  ken acceptance rates among N-gram-based methods, yet its computational bottleneck prevents
  acceptance gains from translating into proportional speedup.


4.2.4   H YBRID A PPROACH : I NTEGRATING T RAINING - BASED AND N- GRAM M ETHOD

• Hybrid speculative decoding can leverage the strengths of different methods. For the hy-
brid approach, we conduct evaluations on SAM[EAGLE-3], which integrates EAGLE-3 and SAM
(detailed hybrid strategies are provided in Section 3.3). As shown in Table 3, SAM[EAGLE-3]
achieves the highest overall speedup ratio across all scenarios. Under the greedy decoding setting,
the SAM[EAGLE-3] demonstrates significant improvements over individual methods. Within the
multi-turn thinking paradigm, SAM[EAGLE-3] delivers speedup gains of 20% and 53% compared
to EAGLE-3 and SAM on QW3-8B, respectively. The reason behind this improvement is straight-
forward: SAM[EAGLE-3] preserves EAGLE-3’s powerful token speculation capabilities while
addressing its limitation in capturing repetitive patterns that emerge during dynamic generation.
• Hybrid speculative decoding retains the limitations of its components. While inheriting
the strengths of SAM, SAM[EAGLE-3] also retains its key drawback, namely, a high sensitivity
to sampling temperature. When the sampling temperature increases from 0 to 0.6, the speedup
ratio of SAM[EAGLE-3] under the multi-round thinking paradigm decreases by 42% on DSL-8B
(3.97×→2.29×) and by 20% on QW3-8B (3.49×→2.79×), respectively. Furthermore, as observed
in Table 3 and Table 4, the performance gain of SAM[EAGLE-3] over EAGLE-3 is less significant.
This indicates that under temperature sampling with T = 0.6, the accuracy of using SAM for
speculative decoding is not satisfactory, leading to the overall performance of SAM[EAGLE-3] being
largely dependent on that of EAGLE-3. Despite certain limitations, the consistent SOTA performance
of SAM[EAGLE-3] across various evaluation scenarios underscores its practical effectiveness. By
integrating the strengths of different methods, the hybrid method achieves well-balanced performance
across various settings, highlighting its potential for reasoning with test-time scaling.

  Takeaway 4: Hybrid speculative decoding uniquely combines the semantic alignment strengths
  of training-based methods with the repetitive pattern capture capabilities of N-gram methods,
  unlocking distinct potential for reasoning with test-time scaling.


4.2.5   M ODEL - BASED M ETHOD

• Model-based method SpS generates high-quality drafts, but faces computational bottlenecks.
As shown in Table 3 and Table 4, SpS achieves the highest MAT. For example, in the multi-round
thinking scenario with QW3-8B (T = 0), SpS’s overall MAT is 1.49 times that of the second-best
method. This superiority primarily stems from SpS employing a smaller, homogenous model as
the draft model, which ensures a high degree of alignment between the output distributions of the
draft and target models. However, this high MAT does not translate into proportional speedup. The
primary bottleneck lies in the computational cost of generating drafts. Despite being smaller, such as
a 0.6B model, the draft model is still too heavy compared to the target models like 8B, particularly
under tight latency constraints. As a result, the time saved by reducing target model evaluations is
largely offset by the overhead of running the draft model, which limits the overall acceleration.

4.2.6                    A NALYSIS OF S PEED S CALING OVER T URNS

• Token N-gram methods achieve progressive acceleration. Figure 2 compares the acceleration
ratios of different methods across reasoning turns. A key observation is that Token N-gram methods
exhibit a steadily increasing acceleration ratio as the number of turns grows. As illustrated in Figure 2a,
SAM and PIA show acceleration ratio increases of 33% (2.13×→2.83×) and 45% (1.45×→2.10×)
in the second turn over the first, respectively. This trend demonstrates their ability to effectively
identify and exploit repetitive patterns that naturally emerge during test-time scaling. Specifically,
by retrieving and reusing relevant intermediate results or reasoning traces from prior turns, these
methods reduce the number of required decoding steps in subsequent turns, leading to progressively
greater efficiency gains over time. In contrast, other methods exhibit nearly constant acceleration
ratios across turns, indicating that each turn is processed independently without leveraging reusable
patterns from prior decoding steps. Further analysis can be found in Appendix A.3.

           Takeaway 6: Token N-gram methods PIA and SAM achieve progressive acceleration by reusing
           past computations, enabling efficiency that scales across turns.



5                     C ONCLUSION

In this paper, we introduce the first benchmark for speculative decoding in accelerating LLM test-time
scaling. Specifically, we evaluate a diverse range of methods, including model-based, training-based,
and N-gram-based approaches, under prominent frameworks such as BoN sampling and multi-round
thinking. Through extensive experiments, we find that simple N-gram-based methods show strong
potential in accelerating test-time scaling by effectively capturing repetitive reasoning patterns. This
highlights the value of integrating N-gram-based methods with other categories of approaches, thereby
balancing acceleration for both repetitive and diverse reasoning in test-time scaling. We hope this
benchmark serves as a foundation for advancing speculative decoding techniques tailored to test-time
scaling, fostering more efficient and practical LLM reasoning.

A.1     A DDITIONAL R ELATED W ORK
A.1.1    T RAINING - FREE F RAMEWORKS FOR T EST- TIME S CALING
Models can acquire reasoning abilities through specialized training that teaches them to generate
long chains of thought internally (Chen et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2025a; Yue et al.,
2025). Complementing these trained capabilities are external test-time scaling frameworks. These
techniques implement an outer control loop around a base model to enhance its reasoning performance
without altering the model’s parameters (Yu et al., 2025b; Yuan et al., 2025; Setlur et al., 2025).
The advantage of these external methods lies in their universal applicability: they can augment any
model’s performance, including those that already incorporate built-in reasoning capabilities. This
work examines two primary categories of external training-free scaling approaches:

• Sampling-Based Methods: The most prominent approach in this category is BoN sampling (Snell
  et al., 2025), where a model generates N candidate outputs, and a verifier or the model itself
  selects the most plausible one. A more sophisticated variant, Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023),
  generates multiple reasoning paths and selects the final answer by a majority vote, demonstrating
  significant improvements in arithmetic and commonsense reasoning. These methods operate on the
  principle that a model is more likely to generate a correct reasoning path than an incorrect one, and
  sampling multiple paths increases the probability of finding a valid solution.
• Iterative Refinement: This class of methods involves a multi-step process where the model
  progressively improves its own output. For instance, Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) enables a
  model to generate an initial response, critique it, and then use that feedback to produce a refined
  answer in a subsequent pass. A more streamlined approach is multi-round thinking (Tian et al.,
  2025), which simplifies the feedback loop. Instead of generating an explicit critique, it provides
  only the final answer from the previous turn as context, prompting the model to re-evaluate its
  conclusion. This method is designed to break “cognitive inertia” by discarding the prior reasoning
  chain, forcing the model to approach the problem anew.

Other search algorithms, such as Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT) (Yao et al., 2023), Graph-of-Thoughts (Besta
et al., 2024), and those based on Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) (Lin et al., 2025a), can be
understood as sophisticated hybrids of these foundational principles. For instance, ToT explores
multiple candidate reasoning steps (a form of sampling) and evaluates them to construct a solution
tree, while MCTS iteratively builds and refines a search policy. By combining multi-candidate
generation with progressive construction and evaluation, they represent a powerful but more complex
class of techniques built upon the core ideas of sampling and refinement.

A.1.2    E FFICIENT R EASONING
While proven effective for unlocking the advanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs, test-time scaling
paradigms are inherently resource-intensive. This significant increase in computational overhead
and inference latency presents a substantial challenge to their practical deployment in real-world,
time-sensitive applications. Efficient reasoning offers a promising path to alleviate this burden by
generating smarter, more concise reasoning with less computation (Wu et al., 2025; Sui et al., 2025;
Xing et al., 2025). Research in this area can be organized into three main categories. (i) Model-based
efficient reasoning (Wang et al., 2025a; Lou et al., 2025), which aims to optimize full-length reasoning
models into more concise variants or directly train compact models specialized for fast and effective
reasoning. (ii) Reasoning output-based efficient reasoning (Hao et al., 2024; Pan et al., 2025; Lin
et al., 2025b), which dynamically reduces the computational work during inference by adaptively
shortening the reasoning chain and eliminating unnecessary steps. (iii) Input prompts-based efficient
reasoning (Ma et al., 2025; Liang et al., 2025), which enhances efficiency by tailoring the reasoning
process according to the properties of the input, such as its specified length constraints.
However, the speculative decoding methods for accelerating test-time scaling examined in this paper
are fundamentally orthogonal to the aforementioned efficient reasoning methodologies (Liu et al.,
2024a), representing a distinctly different paradigm for performance optimization. Recent studies
have also explored speculative approaches within the context of test-time scaling, including but not
limited to speculative acceleration analyses for test-time scaling (Wang et al., 2025b), logit-based
N-gram speculation (Song et al., 2025), and multi-sample inference speculation (Li et al., 2025a).

However, a unified and standardized benchmark for evaluating speculative decoding within the test-
time scaling setting is still lacking, which precisely constitutes the focus of our work. Fundamentally,
speculative decoding constitutes a lossless acceleration technique that preserves the original model’s
output distribution exactly, ensuring that both the quality and inherent characteristics of generated
reasoning remain completely unchanged throughout the acceleration process. This preservation of
distributional fidelity stands in stark contrast to efficient reasoning approaches, which typically achieve
speedup by introducing modifications to the reasoning process itself, whether through aggressive
model quantization that reduces numerical precision (Frantar et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2024; Hao et al.,
2025; Liu et al., 2024b), output compression techniques that sacrifice detail for brevity (Cheng &
Durme, 2024; Hao et al., 2024), structured prompt engineering that constrains the reasoning pathway
(Han et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2025; Aytes et al., 2025), or architectural modifications that streamline
computational flow (Li et al., 2025b; Srivastava et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024).
More critically, speculative decoding operates exclusively at the inference execution level without
fundamentally altering the underlying reasoning methodology, maintaining complete fidelity to the
original model’s cognitive processes, decision-making patterns, and output generation mechanisms.
This approach ensures that no aspect of the original model’s performance characteristics, reasoning
depth, or solution quality is compromised or sacrificed in pursuit of computational efficiency, making
it a particularly attractive acceleration strategy for scenarios where maintaining exact model behavior
is paramount.

A.2      I MPLEMENTATION D ETAILS
All experiments are conducted on a server equipped with a 16-core CPU, utilizing PyTorch 2.1.1 with
CUDA 12.1. The overall evaluation pipeline follows the previous work (Xia et al., 2024). For greedy
decoding with temperature T = 0, we employ exact match verification between the draft model and
target model outputs to ensure generation consistency. For sampling scenarios where temperature
T ̸= 0, we utilize speculative sampling (Chen et al., 2023) for verification, which maintains mathe-
matically proven identical output distributions to the target model. Given that reasoning models may
generate extremely long outputs during their internal deliberation process, we implement specific
constraints to ensure practical evaluation while preserving the models’ reasoning capabilities. We
set the maximum output token limit to 15,000 tokens and establish a thinking budget mechanism
that allows up to 80% of the maximum token allocation for internal reasoning processes. For the
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B model, we implement a forced thinking termination strategy where
designated termination tokens \n\n</think>\n\n are inserted starting from the 12,000th gener-
ated token to compel the model to conclude its reasoning phase and provide the final response. For
the Qwen3 series models, we employ a consistent termination strategy by inserting the official stop-
thinking instruction starting from the 12,000th generated token, following the protocol specified in
their technical report (Yang et al., 2025). Specifically, we employ the designated stop-thinking instruc-
tion, i.e., Considering the limited time by the user, I have to give the
solution based on the thinking directly now.\n</think>.\n\n, to grace-
fully terminate the reasoning phase. Additionally, the detailed information of the open-source

under the BoN scenario. For SAM, to enable batched inference, we modify the suffix automaton’s

reasoning path, and draft tokens across branches are cross-looked-up for matches. While this design
makes batching possible, it inevitably increases the query complexity beyond O(1), representing a
necessary compromise under the current framework. We test BoN with N = 4 and N = 8 on a subset

modified version that supports cross–branch token look-ups). The results suggest that increasing
the batch size indeed tends to trigger compute-bound behavior. For instance, EAGLE-3 achieves
an average speedup of around 1.5× at batch size 4, which is notably lower than its MAT and, in
some cases, even slightly behind the N-gram-based method SAM. This observation is consistent with
our earlier findings, indicating that redundancy plays a crucial role in test-time scaling acceleration.
In practice, when the batch size increases, the speculative size typically needs to be reduced to
avoid becoming compute-bound; the observed slowdown is therefore likely due to the current testing
configuration, which does not carefully calibrate this trade-off. In addition, the parallelization of BoN
inherently reduces overlapping redundancy across different inference paths, which further explains
the diminishing speedup. This reinforces our conclusion that redundancy should be preserved and
fully utilized in speculative decoding. Our benchmark therefore substantiates a key practical insight
that BoN should be applied along the sequential dimension, while the batch dimension should be
reserved for handling different queries to maximize throughput (as leaving the available redundancy
unexploited would be an unacceptable waste).

increasing acceleration ratios as the number of turns progresses. This scaling up and speeding up trend
represents a crucial advantage for test-time scaling acceleration, as it suggests that the computational

subsequent turn becomes progressively faster, making these caching-enabled approaches especially

A.4   P OTENTIAL E XTENSION TO OTHER R EASONING F RAMEWORKS
This work primarily investigates two mainstream test-time scaling frameworks BoN and multi-
round thinking. We argue that other complex reasoning frameworks, such as ToT and MCTS-based
search algorithms, could also benefit from speculative decoding. Conceptually, these frameworks
can be regarded as localized, step-level test-time scaling methods. Instead of generating multiple
complete responses as in BoN or multi-round thinking, these frameworks expand and evaluate
several reasoning paths at each intermediate step. This localized exploration inevitably introduces
substantial overlap and redundancy among candidate steps, a phenomenon closely related to the
computational inefficiency examined in this benchmark. However, leveraging speculative decoding
to address this redundancy in search-based frameworks presents unique technical challenges. A key
distinction makes the direct application of existing speculative techniques to ToT or MCTS-based
reasoning non-trivial. Unlike the relatively linear generation process in BoN or multi-round thinking,
these search-based frameworks involve dynamic operations such as state evaluation, pruning, and
backtracking. Existing speculative decoding methods, whether N-gram-based or advanced variants
using suffix automata (e.g., SAM) or adjacency matrices (e.g., Recycling), are designed for monotonic
forward generation with a single contiguous prefix. Training-based methods such as EAGLE-3 share
the same limitation. Therefore, adapting them to search-based frameworks would require modifying
their underlying write and lookup logic, for example by maintaining separate speculative modules
(such as the individual SAM) for each search step, or extending data structures such as suffix automata
and adjacency matrices to handle deletion operations, since certain steps may be pruned during the
search. In summary, adapting speculative decoding to complex reasoning frameworks is a promising
direction for exploiting redundancy, but it requires non-trivial framework-specific modifications that
fall beyond the scope of this benchmark. We leave this adaptation for future work.



A.5   D ISCUSSION ON H YBRID S TRATEGIES
The simple hybrid strategy benchmarked in this paper demonstrates remarkable effectiveness. If such
a straightforward combination already yields a substantial performance gain, it strongly indicates
the presence of extensive, underutilized redundancy within current architectures. We certainly agree
that developing more dynamic hybrid strategies is a vital future direction. While a comprehensive
exploration extends somewhat beyond the scope of this work, we suggest several promising directions,
including: (i) Context-Aware Switching, where the strategy adapts dynamically based on the
semantic context of the generation task, such as prioritizing N-gram methods for structured outputs
like code while employing training-based draft models for open-ended natural language generation;
(ii) Phase-Specific Optimization, where the strategy is tailored to different stages of the generation
process, for example by utilizing N-gram speculation during a summarization phase where the text is
highly likely to reiterate content from the preceding reasoning steps; and (iii) Uncertainty-Based
Toggling, where the strategy is adjusted on-the-fly according to the model’s predictive uncertainty,
