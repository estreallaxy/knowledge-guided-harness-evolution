A.1. Finite-Sample Variability in Calibration Estimates
The Rogan–Gladen estimator relies on plug-in estimates q̂0 and q̂1 . Figure 7 illustrates their finite-sample variability across
simulation settings. While these estimates are approximately unbiased, their variance can be substantial when the labeling
budget is small. At 1% labeling (m = 20), the positive class may contain only m1 ≈ 3 observations, yielding an RMSE of
0.3 for q̂1 , compared to 0.1 for q̂0 .
Because the RG estimator divides by q̂0 + q̂1 − 1, the resulting confidence intervals can be wide when m is small or when
class imbalance leaves m1 (or m0 ) with few observations.
In contrast, PPI-type estimators and EIF-based approaches do not require explicit estimation of (q0 , q1 ). Instead, they exploit
unbiased correction to learn E[Y | Ŷ ], making them more stable in small-calibration regimes.

A.4. Labeling Mechanisms Beyond MCAR
Table 5 summarizes how common label-acquisition rules map to the missing-data taxonomy. The key distinction is whether
the sampling probability can be written as a function of observed variables. If π(W ) = Pr(R = 1 | W ) is known or
consistently estimable for observed W , the EIF estimator remains valid by using µ(W ) = E[Y | W ] and inverse-probability
weighting. If labeling depends on unobserved Y or latent difficulty, neither π(W ) nor µ(W ) identifies the population mean
without stronger assumptions.
